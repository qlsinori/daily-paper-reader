## Abstract 

Contrastive Language-Image Pre-training (CLIP) learns robust visual models through language supervision, mak-ing it a crucial visual encoding technique for various ap-plications. However, CLIP struggles with comprehending spatial concepts in images, potentially restricting the spa-tial intelligence of CLIP-based AI systems. In this work, we propose SpatialCLIP , an enhanced version of CLIP with better spatial understanding capabilities. To capture the intricate 3D spatial relationships in images, we im-prove both “visual model” and “language supervision” of CLIP. Specifically, we design 3D-inspired ViT to re-place the standard ViT in CLIP. By lifting 2D image to-kens into 3D space and incorporating design insights from point cloud networks, our visual model gains greater po-tential for spatial perception. Meanwhile, captions with accurate and detailed spatial information are very rare. To explore better language supervision for spatial under-standing, we re-caption images and perturb their spatial phrases as negative descriptions, which compels the visual model to seek spatial cues to distinguish these hard nega-tive captions. With the enhanced visual model, we intro-duce SpatialLLaVA , following the same LLaVA-1.5 train-ing protocol, to investigate the importance of visual repre-sentations for MLLM’s spatial intelligence. Furthermore, we create SpatialBench , a benchmark specifically designed to evaluate CLIP and MLLM in spatial reasoning. Spatial-CLIP and SpatialLLaVA achieve substantial performance improvements, demonstrating stronger capabilities in spa-tial perception and reasoning, while maintaining compara-ble results on general-purpose benchmarks.

2.2. Spatial Intelligence in Visual Perception 

Spatial understanding [8, 56] and spatial intelligence are fundamental abilities for artificial intelligence systems to perceive and interact with the complex visual world. In our 3D world, spatial understanding involves distinguishing various spatial relationships between objects, such as up and down, left and right, far and near, and inside and outside, as well as perceiving the size, thickness, length, and width of visual elements. The ability to comprehend and reason on these basic spatial concepts is crucial for solving many complex problems, including robot navigation [70, 75], ma-nipulation [25], human-computer interaction [19, 44], and applications in virtual and augmented reality [33]. MLLMs have made impressive progress in recent years. Built upon pre-trained visual representation and large lan-guage models [20, 59], these methods demonstrate strong performance across a variety of downstream tasks, such as image captioning [39], visual question answering [1], visual grounding [30, 45], and action recognition [23]. However, despite their powerful generalization capabilities, most MLLMs still struggle with spatial understanding and reasoning, as discussed in [7]. There are several works that attempt to address the shortcomings of MLLMs in spatial understanding. Spa-tialVLM [7] is the first to focus on this issue, introducing massive automatically-generated 3D spatial reasoning VQA data for training MLLM. Its successor, SpatialRGPT [12], enhances the 3D-aware data curating pipeline and provides extra visual depth and region information for MLLM. Cube-LLM [14] combines multiple existing 2D and 3D object de-tection datasets in a unified format and then trains MLLM with better spatial detection capability. Existing works mainly enhance the spatial intelligence of MLLM by improving the training data, while our work ex-plores the issue from a different perspective: improving the visual representations. Utilizing the same general-purpose training data, simply replacing the standard CLIP with our SpatialCLIP enhances the spatial understanding capabilities of LLaVA [41, 42]. 29658 Figure 3. ## 3. SpatialCLIP 

Our proposed SpatialCLIP enhances standard CLIP from two perspectives: 3D-Inspired ViT for more powerful “Vi-sual Model” in Sec. 3.1 and recaption&perturbation strategy for more informative “Language Supervision” in Sec. 3.2. 

3.1. Visual Model: 3D-Inspired ViT 

In RGB images, the 3D spatial structure of the world is flat-tened into a 2D plane. Many methods [12, 15, 17, 74] try to introduce depth maps for extra 3D information. However, how to effectively encode such RGB-D inputs while main-taining the pre-trained knowledge for the RGB channel is non-trivial. AlphaCLIP [55] enables an extra input channel with a simple extra input convolution layer, while Spatial-RGPT [12] repeats depth map three times as a depth im-age, and lately fuses the latent of the RGB image and depth image with the pre-trained CLIP image encoder. Our em-pirical findings show that such simple early and late fusion methods are less effective at integrating depth information. To address this, we propose 3D-inspired ViT for RGB-D in-puts to replace the standard vision transformer (ViT) widely used as the visual model in CLIP models. Recently, monocular depth estimation models [31, 65, 66] have gained impressive progress, depth maps can be easily predicted for wild-world RGB images with low cost. Moreover, the accurate depth map shows impressive results in lifting the images into 3D point clouds. Meanwhile, the architectures for processing 3D point cloud [61, 62, 67] fo-cus on capturing spatial relationships among features in 3D space. These observations inspire us to lift 2D tokens into 3D space using predicted depth information and thereby in-corporate design insights from 3D point cloud architectures to better model 3D spatial relationships in images. To this end, we first dive into the standard ViT, which processes RGB images as input. Specifically, an RGB im-age X ∈ RH×W ×3 is divided into 2D patches, and each is linearly projected into C-dimension tokens. The resulting 2D tokens map Z ∈ RHz ×Wz ×C is passed into a stack of transformer blocks, where Hz and Wz represent the tokens map’s height and width. 3.2. Language Supervision: Recaption & Perturba-tion 

In the contrastive learning process of CLIP, visual represen-tations of different images are pulled close with the paired caption while pushed away from captions of other images. The guidance information in language captions strongly in-fluences the visual model’s capability. For prompting 3D-Inspired ViT to better capture spatial relationships in im-ages, more informative supervision from natural language is necessary. However, current widely-used vision-language datasets for training CLIP, such as Conceptual-12M [5] and LAION [50, 51], are inherently noisy and mainly provide simple summaries of global attributes, lacking detailed cap-tion with accurate spatial description. Recent studies [3, 6, 21, 37] have shown that re-captioning images with semantically richer and more aligned descriptions can greatly enhance the performance of vision-language models like CLIP and text-to-image mod-els. However, as discussed in [7, 12], current MLLMs excel at recognizing object categories and attributes but struggle with spatial perception. Consequently, the detailed captions they generated often fail to accurately capture all spatial re-lationships within the image. To address this “chicken-and-egg” dilemma, we propose perturbing the spatial descriptions in the dense captions generated by MLLMs. Specifically, we employ a template to randomly substitute phrases indicating spatial relation-ships with their antonymic counterparts. For instance, a caption like “two cats are standing in front of a table, while the left cat is bigger than the right one” may be modified to 

“two cats are standing under a table, while the upper cat is smaller than the lower one.” 

Since the generated and perturbed captions are semanti-cally consistent in terms of object categories and attributes, applying contrastive learning between these two captions encourages the model to distinguish the entirely incorrect caption from the partially correct one. ## 4. SpatialLLaVA 

As discussed in [57, 58], the capabilities of MLLMs may be constrained by the inherent limitations of their visual repre-sentations. This raises a crucial question: Can the spatial intelligence of CLIP-based MLLM be enhanced by improv-ing the spatial perception of CLIP’s visual representations? 

We utilize LLaVA-1.5, which employs the pre-trained CLIP as its visual encoding model, as the framework to investigate the effectiveness of SpatialCLIP in MLLM. Specifically, we replace the CLIP model in LLaVA-1.5 [41] with our SpatialCLIP. To ensure fair and systematic com-parisons between visual encoding methods, we follow the two-stage training strategy used in LLaVA-1.5 and apply the same training datasets and hyperparameters for each stage. The resulting MLLM is named SpatialLLaVA .

## 5. SpatialBench 

To evaluate the ability of CLIP and MLLM in understand-ing complex 2D/3D spatial relations in images, we build a benchmark SpatialBench , which focuses entirely on spatial perception. Our benchmark employs the caption matching task, requiring CLIP or MLLM to distinguish the caption that best matches the image from several distractors. By manually crafting the correct description and distractors, the caption-matching task requires the model to distinguish nu-anced spatial concepts in the image ( e.g. object arrange-ment, relationships, relative depth, and size). To fully assess models in various scenarios, we collect images of both outdoor and indoor scenes. Specifically, out-door images are collected from the COCO [39] validation set and the Internet, totaling 159 images. Indoor images are sourced from ScanNet [16], consisting of 140 images. Each image is paired with 2 to 4 manually crafted descriptions, of which only one is accurate, with the remaining serving as distractors. ## 6. Experiment 

6.1. Implementation Details 

Training setting. We use images in SA1B dataset [32] for training, since most images in it contain multiple ob-jects and showcase complex spatial relationships. Fol-lowing [6], LLaVA-1.5-13B [41], CoCa [71] and extra ShareGPT4V [9] are used for image reception, the three kinds of captions are randomly used in training for more di-verse and informative supervision. A detailed caption per-turbation template is provided in the Appendix. The tem-perature factor τ in Eq. 3 is set to 0.01. For optimization, we employ AdamW [43] with a learning rate of 2e-7 for pre-trained CLIP encoders and 4e-6 for the newly intro-duced components. Models are trained for 8,000 steps with a batch size of 2,048 on 8 A100 GPUs. 

Model setting. We train SpatialCLIP from OpenAI CLIP-B/16@224px and OpenAI CLIP-L/14@336px, leading to SpatialCLIP-B and SpatialCLIP-L, respectively. We uti-lize the lightweight Depth Anything v2 ViT-Small as our monocular depth estimation model, which brings an extra 24.8M parameters. The kernel size of the 3D CNN in Eq. 1 is 3, and the Dw is 6. The newly added 3D CNN and 3D relative position encoding are both zero-initialized and in-troduce only nearly 1M extra parameters. We develop Spa-tialLLaVA based on SpatialCLIP-L, following LLaVA-1.5.

6.3. Comparison for MLLM 

Results on spatial-focused benchmark. In Tab. 2, we comprehensively validate the spatial perception and reason-ing ability of our SpatialLLaVA on various spatial-focused MLLM benchmarks. With the same training data, Spatial-LLaVA achieves much better results than LLaVA-1.5. Spa-tialLLaVA exhibits a stronger capability to differentiate be-tween nuanced spatial concepts across indoor and outdoor scenes on our SpatialBench. We also conduct absolute performance comparison with more advanced MLLMs, including Spatial-RGPT (trained on spatial-focused data) and LLaVA-1.6/LLaVA-Interleave (trained on much broader general-purpose data). De-spite this ”unfair” comparison, Spatial-LLaVA consistently shows superior spatial perception. Interestingly, Spatial-RGPT excels in tasks it was explicitly trained for (e.g., depth and distance estimation) but underperforms on tasks beyond its training scope (e.g., spatial question-answering). This observation highlights the need to enhance visual per-ception capabilities in MLLMs beyond simply expanding training data. The advantages on BLINK underscore the importance of capturing spatial structure for high-level applications. For “visual corresponding” task, capturing spatial structure is beneficial for identifying the same scene points across dif-ferent viewpoints, lighting, or times, as the scene structure often remains consistent despite these changes. For “Jig-saw,” viewing 2D images in the 3D perspective allows the model to inspect the continuity of the 3D spatial structure rather than only relying on color and texture, aiding in or-ganizing image patches. 6.4. Ablation Study 

Effect of recaption. In the first two rows of Tab. 4, we present the comparison between the original CLIP and its direct fine-tuning using our recaption dataset. On the general-purposed benchmarks, COCO and Flickr30k, tun-ing on our dataset leads to significant performance im-provements, underscoring the benefits of high information density and semantic alignment in recaption data. How-ever, simply using the recaption data only brings negligible changes to the spatial-focused benchmarks, motivating us to introduce more specific designs to learn spatial concepts. 

Effect of 3D-inspired ViT. We ablate the designs of 3D-Inspired ViT in Row 2-5 of Tab. 4. When fine-tuning on the same recaption data, incorporating 3D convolution (Row 3) or 3D relative position encoding (Row 4) shows improved performance on spatial-focused benchmarks compared to the standard ViT (Row 2). The combination of both tech-niques results in even greater enhancements. The improve-ment in spatial perception gained from the 3D-inspired de-sign underscores the importance of integrating 3D point cloud structures into 2D ViT. Encoding images in 3D space with 3D-specific structure enables the visual model to cap-ture complex 3D spatial information more effectively. 

Effect of caption perturbation. The comparison be-tween Rows 5 and 6 in Tab. 4 illustrates the benefits of caption perturbation, which prompts the model to capture spatial concepts and enhances its spatial perception ability. Since MLLMs themselves often struggle in perceiving and reasoning about spatial relationships, the captions generated by them can not fully capture all spatial details. By perturb-ing the spatial phrases, we create references that are fully incorrect in spatial relationships. This approach drives the model to learn spatial concepts by distinguishing the par-tially correct captions from the fully incorrect ones. 

Different depth channel integration strategies. In ad-dition to our 3D-Inspired ViT, there are other alternative methods to enable RGB-D inputs. In Tab. 5, we discuss the commonly used early and late fusion schemes, which project the depth map into RGB features before and after the visual encoder, which are used by AlphaCLIP [55] and SpatialRGPT [12], respectively. In most benchmarks, sim-ply projecting the depth channel to the pre-trained RGB fea-tures is hard to improve the performance. Considering the additional information provided by the reception data, these schemes may even have a negative impact. Conversely, our 3D-inspired ViT makes effective use of the extra depth in-formation, yielding significant improvements. 

## 7. Conclusion 

We present SpatialCLIP to overcome the shortcomings of original CLIP models in spatial understanding. By intro-ducing improved “Visual Model” (3D-Inspired ViT) and enhanced “Language Supervision” (Recaption & Perturba-tion), our model achieves significant improvements, not just on spatial-focused benchmarks like our proposed Spatial-Bench and modified BLINK, but also on zero-shot image-text retrieval. Additionally, we develop SpatialLLaVA based on SpatialCLIP, adhering to the LLaVA-1.5 training protocol.
