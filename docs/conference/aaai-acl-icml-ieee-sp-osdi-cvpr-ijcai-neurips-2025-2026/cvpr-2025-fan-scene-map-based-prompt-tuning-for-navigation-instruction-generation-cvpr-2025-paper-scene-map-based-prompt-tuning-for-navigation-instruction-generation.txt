## Abstract 

Navigation instruction generation (NIG), which provides interactive feedback and guidance to humans along a tra-jectory, is vital for developing embodied agents capable of human-machine communication and collaboration through natural language. Early data-driven methods directly map sequences of past observations to trajectory descriptions on limited datasets, lacking the necessary spatial understand-ing in complex 3D environments. While recent approaches leverage Large Language Models (LLMs) to improve NIG, they often overlook the global spatial context in navigation, such as the inherent space discretization in maps. Instead of straightforwardly feeding textual descriptions of the map into LLMs, we propose a scene map-based prompt tuning framework for NIG, MAPI NSTRUCTOR , which incorpo-rates map context for parameter-efficient updating of LLMs. 

MAPI NSTRUCTOR comprises three key components: (i) 

scene representation encoding, where egocentric observa-tions are projected into 3D voxels for fine-grained scene understanding; (ii) map prompt tuning, which integrates a topological map representation of the entire trajectory into an LLM-based decoder; and (iii) landmark uncertainty as-sessment, which mitigates hallucinations in landmark pre-dictions, thereby enhancing the reliability and coherence of instruction generation. Extensive experiments on three navigation datasets (i.e., R2R, REVERIE, RxR) confirm the generalization and effectiveness of our algorithm.

Second, topological maps enable agents to develop a global perspective of the envi-ronment, facilitating the extraction of crucial spatial cues from the real world. Existing methods [9, 57, 88] incor-porate map information into LLMs through textual descrip-tions. This approach necessitates extensive data annotation and model training while struggling to capture fine-grained scene details in zero-shot or few-shot settings, resulting in the omission of crucial landmarks. To address these challenges, we introduce a Scene Map Prompt Tuning-based Instructor (MAPI NSTRUCTOR ), which incorporates topological map information and fine-grained 3D scene understanding to improve the quality of in-structions. First, rather than relying on compressed seman-tic representations [2, 78], MAPI NSTRUCTOR introduces 3D voxel representations to build a comprehensive scene understanding for each viewpoint. This local 3D represen-tation enables agents to capture more detailed and accu-rate landmarks. Second, MAPI NSTRUCTOR constructs a global scene map for the entire trajectory. Furthermore, MAPI NSTRUCTOR designs a two-stage generation pipeline powered by LLM — landmark prediction and instruction completion. During the landmark prediction phase, MAP-INSTRUCTOR introduces a landmark uncertainty assess-ment, which generates multiple landmark sequences and filters potential landmarks based on a semantic uncertainty score, formalized by semantic entropy [36]. This approach mitigates hallucinations in landmark prediction, thereby en-hancing the instruction completion phase and improving the accuracy of the generated instructions. We conduct experiments on R2R [4], REVERIE [54] and RxR [35] datasets. Experimental results demonstrate that MAPI NSTRUCTOR outperforms the counterparts [17, 20, 34, 61, 70, 75, 76], e.g ., 2.7 % and 4.0 % CIDEr gains on R2R val seen and unseen splits, respectively. 

## 2. Related Work 

Navigation Instruction Generation. Over the past decade, active research has been devoted to interaction between humans and robotics by natural language. Previous stud-ies have spanned various fields, e.g ., instruction follow-ing [20, 61, 75], cognitive psychology [1, 13, 74], and ar-chitectural design [17, 34]. Early studies adopt LSTM-based models to capture complex visual cues and verbal-ize navigation trajectories [20, 61, 67, 70]. Numerous NIG studies demonstrate that improving the linguistic capabil-ities of models can significantly enhance instruction qual-ity [17, 34, 70, 75, 76]. More recently, the emergence of LLMs further empowers NIG models with advanced language understanding and extensive pre-trained knowl-edge [17, 28, 34, 58, 88]. Existing studies [17, 34, 75] predominantly focus on ac-tion and visual information within a single viewpoint, ne-glecting the underlying topological relationships across dis-tinct viewpoints —- an essential factor for precise action de-scriptions and self-localization. Consequently, these meth-ods struggle to capture spatial variation in complex scenes. In contrast, MAPI NSTRUCTOR is incorporated with topo-logical map representations, providing trajectory-level con-textual information that strengthens the agent’s implicit un-derstanding of the environment. 

Scene Representation for Embodied Agents. The abil-ity to understand 3D scenes from 2D observations plays a crucial role in robotic interactions with the physical world. Previous solutions to 3D scene understanding fo-cus on learning the spatial structure of the 3D environ-ment [24, 38, 68], modeling spatial relationships among ob-jects [3, 10, 40, 50], or employing 3D environment recon-struction to obtain a unified representation of the scene lay-out [17, 45, 47]. While metric maps derived from classical SLAM frameworks have long served as the spatial represen-tation for robotic navigation [7, 52, 62], emerging evidence from cognitive psychology challenges this paradigm. Sub-stantial evidence suggests that animals predominantly nav-igate using landmark-based mechanisms [19, 22, 56, 73], relying on a qualitative topological understanding of the environment rather than precise geometric reconstructions. This fundamental discrepancy has driven recent efforts to bridge the gap between geometric and cognitive mapping paradigms. To this end, [56] proposes a semi-parametric topological graph based on observations, yet it lacks ex-plicit object-level connectivity. [32] addresses this limi-tation by incorporating object-aware topological graphs to facilitate contextual reasoning in image-based goal naviga-tion. [5] creates a topological map representation from a vi-sual SLAM map, though this approach imposes significant computational demands on robotic hardware. Despite their outstanding contributions, rare efforts in spatial scene understanding have been devoted to NIG. [17] employs BEV representations to capture object relation-ships within a scene. However, the restricted geometric in-formation in BEV hampers the finer-grained alignment be-tween instructions and visual cues, particularly in complex scenes. Therefore, MAPI NSTRUCTOR enables the seam-less integration of finer-grained discrete occupancy for each viewpoint, capturing more comprehensive geometric details of the scene and enhancing instruction generation. 

Prompting for Multimodal LLMs. With the advance-ment of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), prompt engineering has been a promising approach to har-ness their power. This approach enables control over in-put instructions, allowing MLLMs to generate contextually adaptive outputs without requiring full model retraining. 6899 MLLM

## 3. Approach 

In this section, we first formalize the NIG task setting in §3.1. Next, we introduce a scene representation encoding module in §3.2, which constructs local 3D voxel represen-tations for each viewpoint to enhance scene understanding. We then present a map prompt tuning module in §3.3, which integrates global map information along with trajectories in a prompt-based manner. Furthermore, we propose a land-mark uncertainty assessment pipeline in §3.4, which identi-fies crucial landmarks to improve the accuracy and reliabil-ity of generated instructions.

3.3. Map Prompt Tuning 

In this section, we propose Map Prompt Tuning (MPT), which enables agents to encapsulate both visual appearance and environment connectivity for LLM-based agents. 

Topological Map Construction. Prior studies [46, 68, 87] have shown that navigation agents can readily acquire their global position ( e.g ., via GPS) [6]. Rather than classic NIG methods [17, 20, 61, 70, 75] that rely solely on perspec-tive embeddings as input, the underlying dependencies be-tween global locations are considered and formalized as a graph-structured map in MAPI NSTRUCTOR . Notably, nearby nodes exhibit stronger correlations than distant ones. Thus, we introduce a graph neural network [33] to model node interactions and gather the corresponding spatial in-formation. Concretely, the navigation trajectory is denoted as a directed graph G = {V , E} . Each node v ∈ V in Eq. 7 is augmented with a location encoding and a navigation step encoding. To further incorporate 2D and 3D spatial rela-tionships across viewpoints, the location encoding is de-rived from Euclidean distance d to the previous node in two-and three-dimensional space:

3.4. Landmark Uncertainty Assessment 

The task of NIG requires careful consideration of both the semantic accuracy and the structural coherence of generated instructions. Given the complexity of navigation scenes, such as the presence of diverse and crucial landmarks along the trajectory, generating a varied yet semantically con-sistent set of instructions poses a significant challenge for agents. Inspired by semantic uncertainty in nature language generation [18, 36], we propose a semantic uncertainty-based generation pipeline. Specifically, we decompose the generation process into two stages, i.e ., landmark predic-tion and instruction completion, leveraging the generative strengths of LLMs. During the landmark prediction phase, MAPI NSTRUCTOR generates M sequences of key land-marks {s1, . . . , sM }, and Eq. 11 is reformulated as:

3.5. Implementation Details 

Network Architecture. MAPI NSTRUCTOR is an LLM-based NIG model built upon previous methods [17, 34]. Akin to [17, 34], MAPI NSTRUCTOR employs the Llama-7B [64] model as the initial LLM decoder. The resolution 

(H, W ) of F 2dt,k is (14 , 14) and the 3D spatial resolution 

(X, Y, Z ) of F 3d is (7 , 7, 7) . To model spatial relationships, we adopt GCN [33] to process topological maps. 

Training. For fairness, we follow the same training set-tings as [17], including batch size, optimizers, maximum iterations, and learning rates. Additionally, we utilize syn-thesized navigation route-instruction pairs from PREVA-LENT [26], consistent with previous methods [17, 20, 61, 70, 75]. MAPI NSTRUCTOR adopts a two-stage training strategy, i.e ., landmark prediction and instruction comple-tion. In the landmark prediction phase, MAPI NSTRUCTOR 

uses GPT-4 [53] to extract ground-truth landmarks from the 

train splits as labels. In the instruction completion phase, these ground-truth landmark terms serve as prompts, guid-ing the final instruction generation. 

Inference. During the inference phase, MAPI NSTRUCTOR 

first performs 3 rounds of landmark prediction, and assesses the entropy of landmark semantics (§3.4). τ is set to 0.4. If 

LE (v) > τ , MAPI NSTRUCTOR randomly samples from all landmark predictions to create a new landmark prompt for instruction completion. 

Reproducibility. MAPI NSTRUCTOR is implemented in PyTorch architecture and trained on a single machine with 2 NVIDIA A40 GPUs.

4.5. Instruction Quality Analysis 

To further evaluate instruction quality, we conduct analy-sis experiments following [17, 34], e.g ., path guiding profi-ciency, data augmentation, and user study. 

Path Guiding Proficiency. We generate one instructions per trajectory on REVERIE [54] val unseen using dif-ferent NIG models. All generated instructions are evalu-ated with typical vision-language navigation (VLN) mod-els, e.g ., HAMT [11] and DUET [12]. Following VLN stud-ies [11, 12], we adopt Success Rate (SR) and Success rate weighted by Path Length (SPL) of navigation as evaluation metrics to assess navigation performance. Table 8a presents the performance of instructions generated by different mod-els when tested with HAMT and DUET. Notably, MAP-INSTRUCTOR attains impressive results, with 24.12 % and 

24.63 % SPL on HAMT and DUET, respectively, approach-ing the performance of human-annotated instructions. 

Data Augmentation. Data augmentation, as a classic appli-cation of generators in VLN, is a crucial method for eval-uating the comprehensive performance of different mod-els. As shown in [17, 34], we randomly sample trajectories from the R2R train splits and generate corresponding in-structions using various models as augmentation for EDrop-follower [61]. In Table 8b, MAPI NSTRUCTOR achieves commendable performance across all metrics, yielding ap-proximately 1.1 % and 0.7 % increases in SR and SPL on the R2R val unseen splits. This highlights the high quality of instructions generated by MAPI NSTRUCTOR .

User Study. We study the diversity and semantic align-ment of generated instructions through a human eval-uation study, following the approach in [17, 34, 76]. Concretely, 30 college students are invited to evaluate 100 instructions generated by various models alongside corresponding trajectories of REVERIE val unseen .These instructions are produced by MAPI NSTRUCTOR , C-INSTRUCTOR, BEVI NSTRUCTOR , BT-speaker, EDrop-speaker, CCC-speaker, Lana, and Lana+. Each instruc-tion is rated on a scale from 0 to 5 based on overall performance. As a result, MAPI NSTRUCTOR achieves the highest score, with an average of 4.11 , outperform-ing the other models: BT-speaker ( 3.21 ), EDrop-speaker (3.39 ), CCC-speaker ( 3.56 ), Lana ( 3.74 ), Lana+ ( 3.79 ), C-INSTRUCTOR ( 3.83 ), and BEVI NSTRUCTOR (3.91 ). This confirms the generalizability of our model. 

## 5. Conclusion 

In this paper, we address the challenges of NIG, where pre-vious studies overlook the role of topological maps in spa-tial scene understanding. We propose MAPI NSTRUCTOR ,a scene map prompt tuning NIG framework. By implicitly incorporating topological graph connections across view-points as map prompt features, MAPI NSTRUCTOR achieves promising performance in complex indoor environments. For local scene representations, MAPI NSTRUCTOR designs a 3D voxel representation for finer-grained, object-level de-tection. Moreover, MAPI NSTRUCTOR introduces a land-mark uncertainty assessment pipeline to reduce landmark prediction hallucinations. Experimental results on NIG datasets ( e.g ., R2R, REVERIE, and RxR) validate its supe-riority over existing methods.
